# AI-Native High School & Feeder Platform

Name ideas: AI Native Exeter · AI MIT · Penn M&T for high schoolers

**TL;DR:** An aspirational boarding/day school for grades 9–12 that replaces half the traditional school day with AI-powered individualized learning, paired with a global subscription tutoring platform (pre-K through 8th grade) that serves as both a revenue engine and an admissions funnel. The exit strategy bets that elite tech companies and fellowship programs will recruit directly from the school, making college optional. This will be a feeder program for the future of SPC.

Background: We don't like the Alpha School model.

## 1. The Concept

### Physical School (Grades 9–12)

Starting with a 60-student-per-class campus modeled on the Exeter/Andover mold, but rebuilt around AI. Students spend roughly four hours daily in algorithm-driven, individualized academic work—math, science, writing, coding—with the remaining time devoted to specialized activities (swimming, robotics, music, etc.) coached partly by AI systems. Psychologists and credentialed teachers are on staff to handle the human dimensions: mentorship, emotional development, social dynamics, and the judgment calls that algorithms can't make.

The venture model: subsidize tuition in year one to attract the first cohort, then shift to premium pricing once outcomes data exists. The campus thesis is that empty college facilities (of which there are more each year) can be repurposed.

### Subscription Feeder Program (Pre-K through Grade 8)

A global AI tutoring subscription that scales from 30-minute sessions for kindergartners up to two hours daily by eighth grade. This does three things: generates recurring revenue independent of the physical school, builds a pipeline of students pre-screened and acclimated to AI-driven learning, and creates a standardized assessment layer for high school admissions. It can also be licensed to existing private schools looking to bolt on an AI curriculum.

### Direct-to-Industry Pipeline

Instead of funneling graduates toward college, the school builds placement relationships with companies like Anthropic, Google, and programs like SPC and the Thiel Fellowship. Juniors and seniors take on real projects from partner companies. The thesis is that colleges can't adapt quickly enough to the AI-driven job market, and that a well-trained 18-year-old with two years of practitioner experience will be more valuable than a 22-year-old with a CS degree. Or the new 4 year college could actually be a PhD or medical training program where you get a grant to continue research.

## 2. What's Interesting About This

**The timing is right.** Parental anxiety about AI in education is spiking, but so is demand. Schools are fumbling the transition—banning ChatGPT one year, mandating it the next. A school built AI-native from day one has a positioning advantage that's hard to replicate by retrofitting existing institutions.

**The feeder model is clever.** EdTech subscriptions are hard to retain, but tying them to aspirational school admission creates real lock-in. Parents are willing to pay for years if they believe it's a pipeline to something exclusive.

**The college-bypass angle is a wedge.** Whether or not it fully works at scale, it's a powerful marketing story and reflects a real shift in how top tech companies think about hiring. The Thiel Fellowship has normalized the "skip college" path for exceptional students; this would be the first institution designed to produce them systematically.

**Empty campuses are a real asset opportunity.** Small colleges are closing at an accelerating rate. Converting these facilities into a new type of school is a genuinely smart infrastructure play. Many small colleges have closed since Covid.

## 4. Open Questions for Discussion

**On the school model:**

- What does the 4-hour AI curriculum actually look like? Is there an existing platform being licensed, or does this require building from scratch?
- How does accreditation work for a school with this structure? Which state is the first target, and what are the specific requirements?
- What's the actual student profile? Children of tech workers who already believe in AI? International students? Homeschool families?

**On the subscription product:**

- What's the pricing? How does it compare to existing AI tutoring products (Khanmigo, Synthesis, etc.)?
- Is the feeder-to-school conversion rate realistic? What percentage of subscribers would actually relocate for the high school?

**On the placement pipeline:**

- Has anyone had actual conversations with hiring leads at target companies? Is there any signal that they'd hire from this pipeline?
- What happens for students who don't get placed? Is there a fallback? Can they still apply to college competitively?

**On the team:**

- Who is the founder (or founding team) behind this? What's their background in education, technology, and operations?
- Is there a technical co-founder, or is this still idea-stage?

## 5. Suggested Research Agenda

If the team wants to dig deeper before a decision, here's a prioritized list of things to investigate:

1. **Competitive landscape deep-dive.** Map Alpha School, Synthesis, Primer, Ad Astra/Astra Nova, Khan World School, and any other AI-native or alternative school models. What's working, what's failing, and what's the actual market size for premium alternative education?
2. **Regulatory scan.** Pick 2–3 target states (likely California, Texas, Florida) and map the specific accreditation, teacher staffing, and curriculum requirements. Talk to an education attorney about the liability picture for AI-driven instruction of minors.
3. **Employer pipeline validation.** Run 5–10 calls with engineering hiring leads at target companies. The question isn't "would you hire an 18-year-old?"—it's "under what conditions, and what would you need to see?" Also talk to Thiel Fellowship alumni about what actually happened after they skipped college.
4. **Parent demand signal.** Interview 15–20 parents in the target demographic (tech-forward, high-income, kids aged 10–14). Test willingness to pay, screen time tolerance, and comfort with the college-bypass thesis. This is the fastest way to gut-check product-market fit.
5. **Unit economics model.** Build a bottoms-up financial model for both businesses. Physical school: campus lease/acquisition, staffing (teachers + psychologists + admin), technology costs, tuition pricing at various enrollment levels. Subscription: CAC, LTV, churn assumptions, conversion to physical school.
6. **Campus opportunity scan.** Identify 3–5 recently closed or closing college campuses in target markets. What are they selling for? What's the zoning situation? This could be a meaningful capital advantage if the timing is right.
7. **Funding landscape.** DARPA, NSF, and Department of Education grant programs for AI-in-education funding. Also map which VCs have invested in education recently (Reach Capital, GSV, NewSchools Venture Fund) to understand who might co-invest.
